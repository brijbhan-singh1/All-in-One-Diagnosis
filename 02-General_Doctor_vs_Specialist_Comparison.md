## General Doctor vs Specialist

A General Doctor treats common illnesses and provides primary healthcare, while a Specialist focuses on a particular area of medicine or a specific type of disease.

## 1. General Doctor (General Physician)

- Treats common illnesses such as fever, cold, cough, and infections.
- Helps manage common conditions such as diabetes and high blood pressure.
- Provides initial diagnosis and general medical advice.
- Refers patients to specialists when necessary.

## 2. Specialist Doctor

- Has advanced training in a specific medical field.
- Diagnoses and treats complex or specialised health conditions.
- Examples include cardiologists, neurologists, dermatologists, and orthopaedic surgeons.
- May provide specialised procedures, treatments, or surgery depending on their field.

## Key differences

| Feature | General Doctor | Specialist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main focus | Overall health and common illnesses | A specific medical field |
| Training | Medical degree; may have postgraduate training in general medicine | Medical degree plus specialised training |
| Conditions treated | Broad range of common problems | Specific or complex conditions |
| First point of contact | Usually yes | Often after referral, but direct visits are also possible |
| Cost | Often lower, depending on the clinic | May be higher, depending on the specialist |
| Examples | General physician, family physician | Cardiologist, neurologist, dermatologist |

## Whom should you visit?

- For fever, cough, a common infection, or an unexplained mild symptom: Start with a general doctor.
- For a persistent or complicated problem: A general doctor may refer you to the appropriate specialist.
- For a known condition requiring specialised care: Visit the relevant specialist.
- For a medical emergency: Go to the nearest emergency department immediately.

In simple words: A general doctor is usually your first point of contact for healthcare. A specialist provides focused expertise when a particular condition requires it.
